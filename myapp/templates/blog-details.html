COVID-19 Public Health

Lockdowns Lead to Fewer People Seeking Medical Care: What This Means for Public Health

Dr. Tanay Shah
Chief Medical Officer, WebDoctor
January 1, 2024
8 min read
1.2K views

A recent ICMR study reveals that routine medical consultations in India dropped by 55% during the pandemic peaks, with critical states like Maharashtra and Uttar Pradesh seeing the highest decline. This trend raises alarms for India's 100 million+ diabetic patients and those with hypertension who rely on regular check-ups.

The Indian Healthcare Dilemma

Major hospitals in Mumbai and Delhi reported 60-70% fewer non-COVID OPD registrations. While urban centers gradually adopted telemedicine, rural India faced unique challenges with 40% of PHCs reporting medicine shortages and reduced patient turnout due to transportation barriers.

"Our Anganwadi workers in rural Maharashtra observed a 300% increase in uncontrolled diabetes cases. We must bridge the urban-rural healthcare divide while maintaining COVID protocols." - Dr. Anjali Deshmukh, AIIMS Public Health Expert

At-Risk Groups in Indian Context

Elderly populations in joint families, pregnant women in semi-urban areas, and daily wage workers with limited healthcare access were disproportionately affected. The National Health Portal reported a 22% increase in pregnancy-related complications in tier-2 cities.

Cardiac Emergencies

Mumbai saw 40% fewer STEMI admissions despite similar 911 call volumes, per JJ Hospital data.

Diabetes Management

65% drop in HbA1c monitoring in rural clinics, as per NHM survey

Digital Health Revolution

Indian telemedicine platforms like Practo and Apollo 24/7 witnessed 300% growth, with regional language consultations increasing by 150%. However, only 35% of tier-3 city residents reported satisfactory digital health experiences.

The Road Ahead for India

Integrating ASHA workers with telemedicine infrastructure, expanding Jan Aushadhi generic medicine access, and creating mobile OPD units for slum areas must be priorities. The Ayushman Bharat scheme should evolve to address pandemic-induced healthcare gaps.

About Dr. Rajeshwari Mehta, MD

Dr. Mehta is a Padma Shri awardee with 25+ years experience in community medicine. Former HOD at KEM Hospital Mumbai, she currently advises NITI Aayog on public health strategies and digital health initiatives.
