Diet Plans Comparison

Explore three evidence-based dietary approaches for different health goals

Heart Health Weight Loss Brain Function Plant-Based

Mediterranean Diet

The traditional eating pattern of Mediterranean countries, emphasizing plant-based foods, healthy fats, and moderate animal products.

55-60% Carbs
15-20% Protein
25-30% Fat

Key Principles

  • Abundant plant foods (fruits, vegetables)
  • Olive oil as primary fat source
  • Moderate fish and poultry
  • Limited red meat
  • Moderate wine with meals
  • Regular physical activity

Sample Meal Plan

Breakfast

Greek yogurt with honey, walnuts, and fresh berries

Lunch

Grilled vegetable and hummus wrap with side salad

Dinner

Baked salmon with quinoa and roasted vegetables

Benefits

  • Reduces heart disease risk by 30%
  • Supports brain health and cognition
  • Promotes sustainable weight management
  • Lowers blood pressure
  • Associated with longevity

Best For

Heart health Long-term wellness Diabetes prevention Family eating

Considerations

  • May require adjustment for weight loss
  • Olive oil can be expensive
  • Social drinking component may not suit all

Ketogenic Diet

A high-fat, very low-carb diet that shifts your metabolism to burn fat for energy through ketosis.

70-80% Fat
20-25% Protein
5-10% Carbs

Key Principles

  • Very low carbohydrate intake (<50g/day)
  • Moderate protein
  • High healthy fat intake
  • Focus on whole, unprocessed foods
  • Strict avoidance of sugars and grains
  • Monitoring ketone levels

Sample Meal Plan

Breakfast

Avocado and eggs with bacon

Lunch

Bunless burger with cheese and side salad

Dinner

Salmon with roasted asparagus and hollandaise

Benefits

  • Rapid initial weight loss
  • Reduces blood sugar and insulin levels
  • May improve mental clarity
  • Can improve cholesterol markers
  • Potential benefits for epilepsy

Best For

Rapid weight loss Type 2 diabetes Metabolic health Epilepsy management

Considerations

  • "Keto flu" during adaptation
  • Difficult to maintain long-term
  • Potential nutrient deficiencies
  • Not suitable for everyone

Plant-Based Diet

A diet focused on foods derived from plants, with limited or no animal products.

60-70% Carbs
15-20% Protein
15-25% Fat

Key Principles

  • Fruits and vegetables as foundation
  • Whole grains and legumes
  • Nuts and seeds
  • Minimal processed foods
  • Limited or no animal products
  • Emphasis on food quality

Sample Meal Plan

Breakfast

Oatmeal with chia seeds, berries, and almond butter

Lunch

Buddha bowl with quinoa, roasted veggies, and tahini

Dinner

Lentil curry with brown rice and sautéed greens

Benefits

  • Lower risk of heart disease
  • Healthy weight management
  • Reduced inflammation
  • Higher nutrient density
  • Lower environmental impact

Best For

Heart health Weight loss Diabetes prevention Sustainability

Considerations

  • Need to plan for complete proteins
  • Potential B12 deficiency
  • May require more meal prep
  • Social situations can be challenging
